PETG-CF fan housing modal study

Free-free modal simulation of the cleaned fan housing, with PETG-CF structural material, equivalent motor mass in the front hub, and equivalent electronics mass in the lower base. The engineering objective is to keep structural modes away from the fan operating speed near 2000 rpm and its blade-passing excitations.

Excitation Map

At 2000 rpm, the shaft rotation frequency is 33.33 Hz. If five blades are added, the blade-passing frequency is 5 x 33.33 = 166.7 Hz. The first mode is above 1X rotation, while the third mode is the closest of the first three modes to the five-blade excitation.

This comparison is a screening result. Final acceptance should use the actual motor speed range, the number of blades, and measured or simulated forcing content.

Five-Blade Mass Estimate

The blade estimate uses a mass-only scaling: f_new = f_0 sqrt(M_0 / (M_0 + m_blades)). It does not add blade stiffness, aerodynamic loading, gyroscopic terms, or a rotating assembly model.

For example, adding five blades with a total mass of 100 g would move mode 1 from 49.214 Hz to roughly 47.1 Hz. Heavier blades lower the frequency further, but the current conclusion stays the same for the 1X speed at 2000 rpm.

Circular Gasket Under The Base

Studying a circular gasket is relevant, but only if the support condition is part of the question. In the present free-free modal model, a gasket added only as extra mass would have a small effect. A gasket becomes physically important when it changes how the base is supported by the table.
Fast model Mass ring
Adds gasket mass only. Useful for a quick sensitivity check.
Better model Elastic ring
Adds vertical/lateral spring stiffness and damping under the base.
Best model Rubber contact
Needs gasket geometry, material, contact, and table boundary assumptions.

Drop / Shock Study

A table-drop problem is feasible, but it is not a modal analysis anymore. It is a transient shock or impact study, and the quality of the result depends strongly on contact, material law, and impact duration assumptions.

Simple first approach

  • Choose a drop height, for example 0.8 m.
  • Compute impact speed: v = sqrt(2 g h), about 3.96 m/s for 0.8 m.
  • Replace the impact by an acceleration pulse at the base or by an imposed velocity followed by deceleration.
  • Check peak stresses and deformation trends, not certification-level failure.

More realistic approach

  • Use nonlinear transient dynamics with table contact.
  • Include rubber/gasket damping if present.
  • Use PETG-CF failure allowables from tests or a conservative supplier datasheet.
  • Run several orientations: base-first, ring-first, corner/support-first.
